ADU Fit Check Prototype

Thinking about a backyard cottage or an apartment over the garage? Type your address to see whether an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) could fit on your lot, and what it would take to build one.

This is a prototype, for ideas and discussion. It can show whether an ADU looks possible on your lot. It is not a permit or an approval. The Municipality's permitting and zoning staff need to look at your lot and your plans before you rely on anything here. Results are screening only. Zoning staff make the determination.
What this page can and cannot tell you
  • It checks the main zoning rules for a detached ADU: how far it must stay from each lot line, how much of the lot the buildings may cover, and how big the ADU may be.
  • It does not check the building code, building height, utilities, parking, or anything a surveyor or engineer would measure on site.
  • After an address lookup, the lot lines and buildings come from the Municipality's maps. They are not a survey and may not show recent work.
  • The ADU designs are the Municipality's pre-approved plans. Their sizes on the ground are estimates here, because the plan sheets do not print dimensions.
  • The survey you see before you type an address is a made-up sample.

    How the shadows are worked out

    For thinking and discussion only. No shadow rule is checked. The drawing treats the ground as flat and every building as a flat-topped block at the height in the Municipality's building map, which looks to be the highest point of the roof, so building shadows are a little overstated. Trees and fences are not shown, and they often matter more. The plan sheets print no height, so the ADU height starts as a guess: change it to your own.

    What it takes to build one

    If an ADU looks possible on your lot, these are the steps the Municipality lists for its pre-approved plans.

    1. Pick a design. The pre-approved plans are at muni.org/ADU. You can ask there for a free "not for construction" set to get prices from builders.
    2. Book a free zoning review meeting. Call 907-343-8336. Staff look at your lot with you and confirm whether it is ready for an ADU. Bring the summary from this page.
    3. Call the water and sewer utility early. AWWU Field Services, 907-564-2762.
    4. Get a plot plan. A land surveyor draws your lot with the ADU on it.
    5. Find a builder.
    6. Apply for the permit with the plot plan and the Municipality's forms.
    7. Build it. Inspectors visit during construction, and at the end you receive a Certificate of Occupancy.

    These steps are from the Municipality's Pre-Approved ADU Plans permit checklist (March 2026), which also lists typical costs and times. See muni.org/ADU for the current version.

Check them: some layers are several years old. Well and septic and the driveway question are yours to answer. Results are screening only.

      The rules used for this lot

      An ADU has to stay a set distance back from each lot line (a setback), and all the buildings together may only cover part of the lot. After an address lookup, this page fills in the rules for your zoning district.

      The numbers and code sections, for builders and reviewers

      District values are for a single-family detached home, from Table 21.06-1 in AMC 21.06.020. Chugiak-Eagle River, Girdwood and Downtown have their own tables and are not included. On a lot with more than one street side, every street line gets the full front setback and there is no rear setback (AMC 21.06.030C.4); the zoning director decides the setbacks on such a lot. ADU rules come from AMC 21.05.070D.1: same setbacks as the district, no setback on a side or rear line next to an alley, house plus all accessory buildings within the lot coverage limit, and floor area, not counting a related garage, up to 900 sq ft or 40% of the main house, whichever is greater, never over 1,200 sq ft. The same section limits an ADU to 25 ft in height, or 30 ft over a garage; this page does not check height. In R-1 and R-1A, lots under 10,000 sq ft may go to 50% coverage when the whole house is under 16 ft tall. The gap from buildings is not a zoning rule; set it if your reviewer asks for one.

      Notes for builders and permitting staff
      What the words mean
      • ADU: accessory dwelling unit. A second, smaller home on the same lot as a house.
      • Lot line: the edge of your property. The front lot line is the one along the street.
      • Setback: how far a building must stay back from a lot line.
      • Lot coverage: the share of the lot covered by buildings, counting the house, garage, sheds and the ADU.
      • Plot plan: a surveyor's drawing of the lot showing where the new building would go.
      • Zoning district: the Municipality's label for what can be built in an area, such as R-1.
      Where the map data comes from

      All from the Municipality of Anchorage's public map services, read by your browser. Lot lines, address and zoning district: the assessor's property layer (owner names are never requested). Buildings and their heights: the Buildings layer. Front lot lines and alleys: street and alley centrelines. Seismic zones, FEMA flood areas and AWWU service records fill the extra-steps boxes. Aerial photos are the Municipality's 2026 photo, or 2024 if that does not load. Coordinates are NAD 1983 State Plane Alaska Zone 4, in feet. Nothing you type is stored by this page.
